**Owner rule (2026-07-15), binding on all authenticity work:**

1. **The King James Bible (KJV, KJV.db, and its lineage copies KJVPCE/AKJV/RNKJV/UKJV/Webster) can NEVER be used as a reference for period authenticity.** The KJV *is* the corrupted text under audit — "the word appears in the KJV" is circular evidence and proves nothing about 1611 English. Reasoning like "verified against the actual KJV.db verse text, therefore period" (used in the 2026-07-14 second-opinion pass) is exactly the forbidden move; that pass's conclusions were overruled by the owner for this reason.

2. **The KJV MAY be used for finding similar words**: locating analogous/parallel words and renderings for a rare word — e.g., "what word does the KJV use elsewhere for this same Strong's number?" — is allowed and useful when advising an alternate. Using the KJV as a phrasing/analogy source is fine; using it as an authenticity witness is not.

**Why:** Premise Revision (Decision Log #5) — all written texts are altered-timeline products and merely advisory, and the KJV specifically is the *target* of the alteration. Authenticity judgments must rest on the independent pre-1611 corpora (Wycliffe, Tyndale, Geneva, Middle English texts, the Essayes — themselves advisory but at least not the audited document) and on knowledge of Early Modern English generally.

**How to apply:** when judging whether a word is period, never cite KJV occurrence or a KJV verse as support. If the only ground for "period" would be its presence in the KJV, the verdict is at best unproven — lean `suspect` and advise an alternate found via the KJV's parallel renderings (allowed use) or the period corpora. See also [[corpus-quirks]] for spelling-variant search pitfalls in the independent corpora.

3. **"Well-attested" requires an INDEPENDENT pre-1611 corpus hit, not KJV rarity (owner directive 2026-07-26).** A word that merely appears in the KJV a handful of times — the very rarity that put it on a rare-word review list in the first place — is not thereby "well-attested period vocabulary." That phrasing (used loosely in round 6/7 proposals, e.g. calling a word "well-attested" on the strength of matching Oxford/appearing 2-3 times in KJV.db) smuggles the forbidden KJV-as-evidence move back in through a compliment instead of a citation. A genuine "well-attested" verdict needs a real hit in Wycliffe, Tyndale, Geneva, the Essayes, or a Middle English text — quote the attesting line. Absent that, say "attested only within the KJV corpus itself, which is not authenticity evidence" and lean `suspect`, not "well-attested."

**Exception (owner directive 2026-07-26):** a **proper name, musical/technical instrument, genealogical term, taxonomic/scientific-type name of a creature or plant** may still be called well-attested on weaker grounds than ordinary vocabulary — namely, if **other translations** (Geneva, Tyndale, Wycliffe, or the modern witness set in `rare_word_witnesses`) independently use the same or a cognate rendering for that referent. These are closed-class, referent-bound words (there is usually no ordinary-English synonym for a named instrument, a specific bird/beast, or a kinship/lineage term), so cross-translation agreement on the referent substitutes for a plain pre-1611 corpus hit. This does not relax the rule for ordinary nouns/verbs/adjectives — it only recognizes that naming-words are attested differently than descriptive vocabulary. State which category applies (name / instrument / genealogy / creature-or-plant type) and which other translation(s) agree, then it may carry WHITELIST or "keep" advice.
