## Current English aka New Modern English (NMoD) Inflection

English inflection refers to changing the form of a word (usually by adding a suffix) to express different grammatical categories like tense, number, or ownership. Unlike some languages with complex case systems, English relies heavily on these small changes to verbs and nouns.

Here is a breakdown of the three areas you asked about:

| Category | Base Form | Rule | Inflected Form | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Tense** | walk | Base / +s | walk / walk**s** | Action now |
| **Past Tense (Reg)** | play | +ed | play**ed** | Finished action |
| **Past Tense (Irreg)** | go | Change | **went** | Finished action |
| **Past Participle** | eat | Change | **eaten** | Perfect tenses |
| **Present Participle** | run | +ing | runn**ing** | Continuous action |
| **Possessive (Singular)** | cat | +'s | cat**'s** | One owner |
| **Possessive (Plural s)** | dogs | +' | dog**s'** | Many owners |
| **Possessive (Plural x)** | children | +'s | childr**en's** | Many owners |
| **Plural (Noun)** | box | +es | box**es** | >1 item |
| **Plural (Noun)** | baby | +ies | bab**ies** | >1 item |

Key:

**Regular Verbs**: Always use -ed for past tense.
**Irregular Verbs**: Change spelling entirely (e.g., sing → sang).
**Apostrophe (')**: Indicates ownership, never used for simple plurals (e.g., "dog's" = owns; "dogs" = many).

## Early Modern English (EModE) Inflection 

| Category | Base Form | Rule (EME) | Inflected Form | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present (2nd Sing)** | walk | +st / +t | walk**st** | "Thou walk" |
| **Present (3rd Sing)** | walk | +th | walk**th** | "He walketh" |
| **Past Tense (Reg)** | play | +ed / +t | play**ed** / play**t** | Finished action |
| **Past Tense (Irreg)** | speak | Change | **spake** | Finished action |
| **Past Participle** | break | +en / Change | **broke** / **broken** | Perfect tenses |
| **Present Participle**| run | +ing | runn**ing** | Continuous action |
| **Possessive (Sing)** | king | +es / +s | king**es** / king**s** | One owner |
| **Possessive (Plur)** | kings | +es | king**es** | Many owners |
| **Plural (Noun)** | man | +en / Change | **men** | >1 item |
| **Plural (Noun)** | child | +ren | childr**en** | >1 item |


**Verbs**: Second person singular (thou) takes -st or -t. Third person singular often takes -th (instead of just -s).
**Past Tense**: Regular verbs still used -ed, but spelling varied (e.g., -t, -d). Irregular verbs had different forms (e.g., spake instead of spoke).
**Possessive**: The apostrophe was not standard for possession; -es or -s was added directly to the noun (the apostrophe was often used for contractions or omitted entirely).
**Thou vs. You**: Thou (singular, informal) triggered the -st ending. Ye/You (plural or formal) used the base verb (like modern "you").
**The "-eth" ending**: The third-person singular often ended in -eth (e.g., he doth, she hath) rather than the modern -s.
**Possessives**: The apostrophe 's was not a standard grammatical marker for possession yet. You would see the kinges sword (Shakespeare) rather than the king's sword. The apostrophe was primarily used for contractions (e.g., o'er for over).
**Irregular Past**: Many verbs that are regular today were irregular then, and vice versa (e.g., helped was often holp).

# The Second-Person Pronoun Work — Findings, Choices, Decisions (2026-07-26)

This part records what was measured, what was chosen, and what was ruled during
the pronoun passes of 2026-07-26. It answers the open questions raised in the
sections above — in particular *"Should thine even be used?"* under
**Your → Thy / Thine**. Roadmap Decision Log entries **#17** and **#18** carry
the same rulings in the project's decision record.

## 1. The paradigm at issue

Early Modern English marks second-person **number** and **case** in the pronoun
itself — a distinction Present-Day English no longer has. The 1611 translators
used it deliberately, to mirror Hebrew and Greek, both of which distinguish a
singular from a plural "you".

| | nominative (subject) | oblique (object) | possessive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **singular** | thou | thee | thy / thine |
| **plural** | ye | you | your |

Two further rules govern the possessive and the reflexive:

- **thy / thine splits on the following sound**, exactly as *a / an* does —
  **thine** before a vowel or *h* (*thine eyes, thine heart, thine hand, thine
  own*), **thy** before a consonant (*thy God, thy father, thy land*).
- **thine is also the absolute possessive** — a pronoun in its own right rather
  than a modifier: *"that which is **thine** with me"* (Gen 31:32), *"**thine**
  they were"* (John 17:6). In this use it stands whatever follows.
- **thyself** is the attested EModE reflexive. There is no *thyselves* in 1611
  English; the only attested plural reflexive is *yourselves*.

**So: yes, "thine" belongs in Early Modern English.** It is not a Middle
English survival that Oxford failed to finish updating. It is the live EModE
pre-vocalic and absolute form, and it is used that way throughout.

## 3. Decisions

**Decision 1 — no form of "you" remains (owner ruling 2026-07-26).**
The owner has **no memory of "you" or "your" in the bible** and holds that the
KJV read *ye / thou / thee / thy / thine* throughout. Under the Premise
Revision (Decision Log #5) **memory testimony outranks every corpus in this
project, the base KJV included**; the source-language agreement in §2 is
advisory and cannot veto a memory. It is recorded so the reasoning stays
re-derivable, not as a counter-argument.

The owner's rationale: in Present-Day English a second person addressed to a
group is understood from context — *"I have no problems among you"* — so a
number-neutral oblique reads correctly to a modern ear.

**Decision 2 — the conversion was already case-accurate, so no case fixes were
made.** Because every pre-pass "you" was oblique (§2) and **thee** is the
oblique form, `you → thee` matched case exactly. Nothing needed promoting to
*thou* or *ye*, and no verb agreement was disturbed. *thou* (5,479) and *ye*
(3,979) were never touched by any pass.

**Decision 3 — the resulting system, stated.** Number is marked where EModE has
distinct nominative forms and collapses where the context principle carries it:

| | singular | plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **nominative** | thou | **ye** |
| **oblique** | thee | thee |
| **possessive** | thy / thine | thy / thine |
| **reflexive** | thyself | thyself |

A consequence worth naming: 1,416 verses now pair plural *ye* with *thee* or
*thy* in one sentence — Gen 17:10 *"this is my covenant, which **ye** shall
keep… every man child among **thee** shall be circumcised."* Under this
paradigm that is **not** an inconsistency; it is nominative-plural plus a
number-neutral oblique, which is what the scheme intends.

**Decision 4 — thy before consonants, thine before vowels and h.** The first
pass mapped every `your → thine` without the phonological split, producing
1,357 forms no 1611 printing would set (*thine God* 182×, *thine fathers* 88×,
*thine father* 40×, *thine brethren* 38×, *thine children* 33×). These were
corrected to *thy*. **Vowel- and h-initial followers were never touched**, and
the **6 absolute possessives were preserved as *thine*** — Gen 31:32,
Deut 15:3, Deut 30:4, I Chr 21:24, Jer 32:7, John 17:6.

**Decision 5 — the two survivors of whole-word matching.**

- `yours → thine` (12) — the attested absolute possessive. Luke 6:20 now reads
  *"for **thine** is the kingdom of God"*, landing on the same formula as
  Matt 6:13.
- `yourselves → thyself` (188; owner ruling) — *thyself* is attested where
  *thyselves* would have been a coinage. Number is carried by context and by
  the *ye* standing in the same sentence: Gen 49:2 *"Gather **thyself**
  together, and hear, **ye** sons of Jacob."*

**Reconstructed KJV in Modern English** (`custom/site-modern.json`) — a second
export for readers who want the reconstruction without the archaic forms. It
takes the same restored base and applies the built-in modernization layer of
`scripts/custom_export.py` (262 rules, ~47,000 replacements per run), which
**reverses the pronoun work and modernizes the inflections**:

| Early Modern | Modern |
| :--- | :--- |
| thee, thou, ye | you |
| thy, thine | your |
| thyself | yourself |
| -eth (he walketh, he hath, he doth) | -s (he walks, he has, he does) |
| -est / -st (thou walkest, thou hast) | base form (you walk, you have) |

The `-eth` / `-est` rule is applied automatically (1,779 replacements) but only
fires when a base form of the word is itself a word in the text — the guard,
plus `ETH_BLOCKLIST` / `EST_BLOCKLIST`, is what protects *priest*, *harvest*,
*forest*, *Nazareth*, *greatest*, *twentieth*. Loosening it silently corrupts
nouns and names.

The modern edition therefore contains **0** instances of *thee, thou, thy,
thine, ye, thyself* in its scripture text (the Restoration Appendix still shows
them in its `was:` lines, which is the intended audit trail).

### Known limitation — the absolute possessive in the modern edition

The modernization map is word-for-word and cannot see context, so it renders
*thine* as *your* in **all** positions. In the 37 verses where *thine* is the
**absolute** possessive, Present-Day English requires ***yours***, and the
modern edition currently reads *your*:

- Luke 22:42 — "not my will, but **your**, be done" → should be *but **yours***
- I Chronicles 29:11 — "**Your**, O Lord, is the greatness" → ***Yours**, O Lord*
- I Corinthians 3:21 — "For all things are **your**" → *are **yours***
- John 17:10 — "all mine are **your**, and **your** are mine" → *are **yours**… **yours** are mine*
- Matthew 6:13 — "For **Your** is the kingdom" → *For **Yours** is the kingdom*

This does not affect the original-voice edition, where the absolute *thine* is
correct as it stands. Fixing it needs a context-sensitive rule in the engine —
"*thine* not followed by a word it modifies → *yours*", the same absolute test
already used by `83_thine_to_thy_before_consonants.py` — because a settings
`GlobalReplacements` entry maps a word to a word and cannot make the
distinction. **Open; not yet ruled.**
