## Borderline / not recommended

- **graffed** (item 457, Romans 11:17-24) — horticultural referent (grafting an olive branch) would fit "plant" if it were a noun, but the word is a verb (past tense of "graff/graft"); excluded per the owner's verb rule.
- **shorn** (item 327, Song of Solomon 4:2; I Corinthians 11:6) — sheep-shearing is an animal-husbandry action tied directly to the animal referent, but the word functions as a verb/participle here, not a noun naming the creature; excluded.
- **flay / flayed** (item 231, Leviticus 1:6; II Chronicles 29:34; Micah 3:3) — describes removing an animal's skin; animal-adjacent but the word is a verb, not the animal itself; excluded.
- **crew** (item 418, Matthew 26:74 and parallels) — the cock's crowing is an animal-adjacent action, but "crew" here is the past-tense verb "crowed," not a creature noun; excluded.
- **muzzle** (item 42, Deuteronomy 25:4; I Corinthians 9:9; I Timothy 5:18) — an animal-husbandry term ("thou shalt not muzzle the ox"), but functions as a verb in all three references (the noun sense, a device, is not itself an animal name); excluded.

## Category-ambiguous items considered and left unrecommended (not verb-related)

These touch one of the five categories loosely enough that they are reported here for transparency, but were judged not to clearly fit the category as defined (kept out of the main table rather than the verb-exclusion list):

- **stalk** (item 336) — names a plant *part* (the stem bearing grain), not a species/type name; parallel to excluding body-part words like "teats," "navel," "paps," "fins."
- **blade** (item 394) — mixed senses across its five references: a plant shoot in Matthew 13:26/Mark 4:28, but a sword/farm-tool blade in Judges 3:22 and Job 31:22; the inflection group as a whole does not cleanly name a plant.
- **firstripe** (item 227) — describes early-ripening fruit but is an adjective of timing, not a plant/crop type-name itself.
- **paradise** was the only clear "location" hit in this range; other place-adjacent common nouns (ponds, brink, clefts, thickets, marketplace, sheepfolds, landmark, conduit) were judged to be ordinary generic vocabulary rather than named geographic referents, and were left unrecommended.
