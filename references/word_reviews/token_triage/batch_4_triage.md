## yarn — 7 uses
- verdict: REPLACE
- suggestion: [whitelist] none fits — no whitelisted word carries a place-name sense; "linen" (the nearest textile term) fails because the referent in the flagged verses is not a textile at all. | [witness] Wycliffe 1382, I Kings 10:28: "the horsis of Salomon weren led out of Egipt, and of Coa; for the marchauntis of the kyng bouyten of Coa" — and II Chronicles 1:16 (Wycliffe): "horsis weren brouyt to hym fro Egipt, and fro Choa." Assyrian *qēwē*) recognized in modern Hebrew lexicons as the likely referent of מִקְוֵה in this phrase.
- reason: Flagged only at I Kings 10:28 and II Chronicles 1:16 (Solomon's horse-and-chariot trade) — a textile word sitting incongruously in a passage entirely about horses, the classic "bottles" signature: a well-attested 1611 English word (Geneva itself uses a cognate) naming the wrong kind of thing for its context. The Exodus 35:25 use of "yarn" (spun thread for tabernacle curtains, a genuine textile context) is sound and NOT part of this flag. Because the corrected reading is a proper place name absent from the base text entirely, this needs owner review rather than a simple word-for-word swap.
