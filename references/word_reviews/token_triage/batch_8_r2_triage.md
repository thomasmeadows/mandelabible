Whitelist candidates were checked against the anchor-link list in `references/word_whitelist.md`. **Verdict tally: 108 KEEP, 9 WHITELIST, 0 REPLACE.** No corruption signal (post-1611 coinage, source-era anachronism, or meaning inversion) was found in this batch's vocabulary itself — these are ordinary, well-attested Early Modern words that simply fall below the whitelist's frequency floor. Two verse-level oddities worth the owner's separate attention are flagged in the closing notes: Genesis 43:3's "straitly protest" and Genesis 31:34's "camel's saddle" both diverge from the standard 1769 Blayney wording I recall ("solemnly protest," "camel's furniture") — that is a verse-restoration question, not a vocabulary problem, and is reported for the record rather than ruled on here.

## straitly — 13 uses
- verdict: KEEP
- whitelist: "diligently" — Deuteronomy 6:17 "Ye shall diligently keep the commandments"; common intensifying adverb, dozens of KJV uses.
- witness: no witness reads "straitly" at Genesis 43:3. Geneva 1599: "The man charged vs by an othe" (built of whitelisted words: charged, oath); Tyndale: "the man dyd testifie vnto vs saynge"; Wycliffe: "denounside to vs vndir witnessyng of an ooth." All three paraphrase away from a "straitly"-type adverb toward an oath-verb construction.
- own: keep "straitly" — it is genuine, well-attested 1611 vocabulary (Exodus 13:19 "he had straitly sworn," Genesis 43:7 "asked us straitly of our state" are both standard KJV wording).
- reason: "Straitly" (= strictly, urgently) is sound EModE and the underlying referent (an urgent oath/charge) is source-era. "Diligently" and Geneva's oath-phrase are both worse than the word standing because they lose the specific sense of stern insistence that "straitly" carries in legal/oath contexts. Note for the owner: the Genesis 43:3 occurrence itself reads "did straitly protest," which I recall differs from the standard 1769 text's "did solemnly protest" — a verse-level question, separate from this token's vocabulary ruling.

- witness: Geneva 1599 (Deut 17:8) reads "betweene plague and plague" where the base text has "stroke and stroke" — a real divergence; Wycliffe reads "lepre and not lepre" (disease-reading of the same Hebrew nega`). Neither witness uses "stroke" here.

"Wound" is close but narrows the sense (a stroke need not break skin); Geneva's "plague" reflects a different construal of the same Hebrew root and would import disease-language where the English tradition (KJV, Wycliffe partially) reads a blow/case.

## unjust — 19 uses
- verdict: KEEP
- whitelist: "wicked" — extremely common near-synonym, and paired directly in the same verse ("the deceitfull and vnmercifull people" / base's "deceitful and unjust man," Ps 43:1, in this group).
- witness: Geneva 1599 (Ps 43:1) reads "the vnmercifull people" for the base's "unjust man" — a real, close divergence.
- own: keep "unjust" — genuine EModE adjective, standard KJV usage (also Prov 11:7, 28:8, both in this group), naming specifically a violation of justice/equity rather than general wickedness or unmercifulness.
- reason: Geneva's "unmerciful" shifts the sense toward lack of compassion rather than injustice specifically (a distinct moral category — one can be just but harsh, or merciful but corrupt); "wicked" is broader still. "Unjust" precisely names the legal/ethical failing (perverting judgment, the concern of Prov 11:7, 28:8) and is preferred.

## accursed — 20 uses
- verdict: KEEP
- whitelist: "cursed" — extremely common, cognate root word.
- witness: no witness at Deuteronomy 21:23 uses "accursed"; Wycliffe reads "he that hangith in the cros is cursid of God" (cursed, the simple cognate), Geneva reads "the curse of God is on him."
- own: keep "accursed" — genuine intensive EModE form of "cursed," standard KJV usage, notably including the technical sense "devoted to destruction" (the herem/ban category, Josh 6:17-18, in this group — a distinct legal-cultic status from ordinary "cursed").
- reason: Wycliffe and Geneva both prefer the simple "cursed," but the KJV's own text uses "accursed" specifically for the herem/ban category at Joshua 6:17-18 (a devoted-to-destruction status under the conquest law, distinct from an ordinary curse) — collapsing "accursed" into "cursed" would blur that specific legal-cultic distinction which this same group's verses depend on.

## alas — 20 uses
- verdict: KEEP
- whitelist: "woe" — extremely common near-synonym, though "woe" typically opens a pronouncement of judgment while "alas" is a cry of personal grief/dismay — a real register difference.
- witness: no witness at Numbers 12:11 uses "alas"; Wycliffe drops the interjection entirely ("he seide to Moises, My lord, Y beseche"), Geneva keeps "Alas, my Lord" (identical word).
- own: keep "alas" — Geneva agrees outright; a genuine EModE interjection of personal grief/dismay, well attested (also Num 24:23, Josh 7:7, both in this group).
- reason: Geneva is unanimous with the identical word; "woe" is available but is the KJV's own word for a formal pronounced judgment or lament-oracle ("woe unto thee"), a different speech-act from "alas," the personal cry of dismay Aaron and Joshua both utter — the word standing preserves that distinction.
